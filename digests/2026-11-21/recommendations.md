## 1. Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation (ROGUE-AI)

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26553v1
- **摘要**: Agents based on large language models (LLMs) can now delete files and send payments on behalf of their users. News media reports routinely describe failures of these agents (e.g., Replit's coding agent deleting a production database), but a report often only says what went wrong and rarely (in)validates the incident or finds its root cause. Incident catalogs collect these reports, and benchmarks record whether agents fail, but neither replicates an incident nor tests the cause its report might identify. In this paper, we present ROGUE-AI (Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation), a multi-agent framework that closes the above gap and rebuilds each reported incident offline together with a matched control that lacks only the blamed cause, and runs agents on both. Our results suggest that ROGUE-AI is able to reproduce the reported harm in 89% of its 108 attempted replications. Agents built on five current GPT, Claude, Gemini and DeepSeek models made the reported mistake in 18% to 33% of runs, against at most 1% without the blamed cause. Of the 72 incidents with real-world harm, 62 had no underlying attacker, and in these, agents did what their task asked for but missed an ordinary hazard, such as a database shared with the live site. Replications without an attacker caused more harm than replications of prompt injection for all five models. Defenses against prompt injection look for injected instructions, but these replications contain none. Further, a warning after each tool output that names the hazard and its consequence lowered harm for all five models, whereas a note of the same length that does not mention the hazard left most of the harm in place. Built from real incidents, the ROGUE-AI replications form a reusable audit framework that vendors can rerun before each new release.

## 2. The Geometry of Lies: Knowledge and Persona Directions for Detecting Deception in Large Language Models

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26545v1
- **摘要**: Linear probes are the cheapest LLM lie detectors but often fail to transfer across kinds of lie. Label-free directions from honest and deceptive role-play are untested at 70B scale and on lies about knowledge, where a lie and an honest denial can be identical. We widen a published persona bank with roles that deny knowledge they have, plus honest counterparts. At each model's middle layer the resulting persona direction ranks lies across Liars' Bench better than single-dataset probes on three of its four models; it fails on Qwen, even when Qwen stays in role far more often. On Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct it beats directions from a random persona bank, but its gain over the published direction is within the range of random widenings. On lies about knowledge, persona directions mainly track a reply's wording: where lies and honest denials share their words, our three main contrasts stay inside the random-direction band on Llama and Qwen and reach only its edge on Gemma, though on Mistral one clears it. A knowledge direction contrasts questions the model can answer with ones nobody can, each followed by the same scripted denial. It separates lies from identical honest denials on two models and narrowly on a third, but not on the fourth; its other results partly depend on the benchmark's wording. On the Gender-Secret fine-tune it clears the band by a few hundredths on Llama and fails on Mistral. The sum of the two directions ranks lies better than a supervised probe at the same depth on all four models (on one, both near chance) but is level with the persona direction on Llama's average and behind that probe on held-out lies. More varied role-play questions do not help the persona direction on Liars' Bench.

## 3. AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26613v1
- **摘要**: Frontier multimodal language agents such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated strong capabilities in controlling robots directly via low-level numerical actions without high-level primitives. However, existing benchmarks report only episode-level success rates, which show neither how these agents succeed nor where they fail. In this paper, we present a systematic action-level analysis of frontier agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. We introduce AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework in which the policy agent states a local intent alongside each action, and a critic evaluates the action's outcome and failure mode against this intent. Our action-level analysis demonstrates the promising capabilities of frontier agents on direct robot control, including closed-loop recovery from local execution failures, reliable semantic grounding across diverse scenes, and strong zero-shot manipulation without task-specific adaptation. It also identifies their current limitations, including task-relevant physical execution bottlenecks such as grasping and alignment, and uneven recovery across failure modes. Finally, we validate our critic on a 400-action human-annotated benchmark and release a dataset of 12,844 annotated actions alongside a fine-tuned open-source critic, Muse-Glimmer-30B, whose failure-mode accuracy rises from 32.58% to 74.24%, enabling low-cost action-level analysis.

## 4. AutoMaster: Autonomous Post-Training by Making the Same Model a Better Teacher

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26510v1
- **摘要**: Task success alone does not specify which behaviors post-training should reinforce. Even successful teacher trajectories can contain unsupported assumptions, unproductive repetition, or superficial verification. We propose Skill-FT (Skill-Guided Fine-Tuning), which uses natural-language skills to suppress undesirable habits and elicit underrepresented effective behaviors during teacher rollouts, then fine-tunes a target model on the resulting trajectories. We introduce AutoMaster, an autonomous post-training system in which an LLM agent develops these interventions through trajectory analysis, skill design, data selection, and feedback from rollouts and target-model evaluation. Across Terminal, Search, and software engineering tasks with 4B and 9B target models, AutoMaster improves mean performance over Vanilla SFT on successful unguided teacher trajectories by 1.42-8.99 percentage points, with the teacher, task pool, training set size, and SFT recipe held fixed. Together with the ablations and trajectory analyses, these results suggest that behavioral shaping can make a fixed model a more effective teacher. We further find that retaining skill instructions during SFT yields higher mean performance than removing them across all six settings, despite their absence at evaluation and identical supervised outputs. These findings suggest that preserving skill context as part of a training demonstration can support better behavioral internalization.

## 5. Multimodal Error Detection in Chest X-ray Reports: A Large Language Model Reading Image Evidence

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26492v1
- **摘要**: Rad-Linter checks chest X-ray reports against image-derived evidence to flag potential errors for review. A pretrained image classifier supplies scores for nine pathologies, and a 1.5B-parameter language model, fine-tuned with LoRA on 1,816 examples, checks their consistency with the report. We compare it with a keyword-and-negation rule baseline given the same reports and scores. Evaluation uses controlled report-score pairings from OpenI, with consistency labels derived from automatic report labels and score thresholds. Each counterfactual quad crosses two real reports with two real score vectors from other patients, creating two consistent and two inconsistent pairs. Every report and score vector occurs in both conditions, so either input alone permits only 50% accuracy. On 131 quads, Rad-Linter achieves 98.5% pair accuracy versus 80.5% for the rule baseline, and classifies all four pairs correctly in 94.4% of quads versus 56.5%. The checkers perform similarly on omission items, including unmentioned and negated findings; larger differences occur on the remaining item types, which require interpreting how findings are described. The same language model without fine-tuning remains near 50% accuracy. On a second set of 181 quads that also includes reports on which the automatic labelers disagree, pair accuracy is 93.4% versus 75.3%. These controlled results show that, given the same image-derived scores, a trained language model detects report-score inconsistencies more accurately than the tested rule baseline, with the advantage concentrated in interpreting how findings are worded.
