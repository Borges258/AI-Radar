## 3. The Indian Financial Automated Advice Bias (IFAAB) Dataset : Auditing Socioeconomic and Demographic Disparities in LLM-Generated Financial Advice in India

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38239v1
- **概述**: 该数据集审计了印度背景下LLM生成的金融建议，揭示了社会经济和人口统计特征导致的建议偏差与不平等现象。
- **摘要**: Large language models are increasingly used for financial guidance, yet little is known about whether their advice changes across social identities in Global South contexts. We introduce the Indian Financial Automated Advice Bias (IFAAB) benchmark, a counterfactual audit dataset of 31,734 GPT-4o-generated financial-advice responses for synthetic Indian personas varying by gender, caste, education, employment, residence, age, and district. Holding the underlying request fixed, we evaluate disparities in LLM-implied wealth, income, savings, projected gains, textual similarity, advice topics, and qualitative bias judgments. In the gender-only audit, male-labelled profiles receive 60.1\% higher initial total resources and 46.8\% higher post-advice total resources than comparable female-labelled profiles; in the caste-augmented audit, the post-advice total-resource gap remains 49.4\%. Adding district information reduces the aggregate post-advice total-resource gender gap to 7.9\%, revealing strong geographic conditioning. Standalone caste comparisons also show systematic stratification: relative to General-category profiles, OBC, SC, and ST profiles receive significantly lower post-advice total resources, respectively. Textual audits show low semantic similarity between paired gendered responses, with an average TF--IDF cosine similarity score of 0.44. Qualitative analysis shows that women are more often steered toward cautious saving, community support, and basic financial literacy, while men receive more growth-oriented advice involving investment, entrepreneurship, and asset accumulation. IFAAB provides a reusable benchmark for evaluating fairness, suitability, and consumer-protection risks in LLM-mediated financial advice, and highlights the need for locally grounded, intersectional evaluation of AI systems in high-stakes economic domains.

## 4. Disparity Laundering: Consensus Without Fairness in Clinical Multi-Agent Systems

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38215v1
- **概述**: 研究表明临床多智能体系统通过共识机制虽能降低错误率，但未能消除模型偏见，甚至可能因群体效应加剧公平性缺失。
- **摘要**: Clinical large language models are increasingly fielded as multi-agent teams, on the assumption that deliberation averages out individual error. It does not average out bias. Across a full factorial of five model families and four aggregation topologies on 120 real MIMIC-IV emergency cases under counterfactual demographic swaps, no configuration neutralizes the disparity: all 20 cells commit the direction of their agents' independent answers, giving Black patients more acute triage. Every model has a topology that amplifies its baseline gap, but which one varies by model, so no design is reliably safe or reliably amplifying: the disparity is relocated, never removed. Late-stage fixes fail: fairness instructions and demographic masking leave the gap untouched, and per-agent self-debiasing widens it, because the bias is already encoded in the agents' ordinary clinical numbers. We call this disparity laundering; the audits now applied to clinical AI, per model, per output, per version, can certify the parts but never the committed decision. Interventions close the gap exactly when they make counterfactual versions visible inside the system and pool them, through deliberation, into the committed decision. A diverse-perspective ensemble does both while keeping the team, with no retraining or architectural change: the disparity falls by 67 to 94% where all five perspectives pool at every round, and the one topology that never pools them is the one it cannot reliably repair. Medical multi-agent systems do not filter demographic bias; they launder it into plausible decisions no single-version audit can catch, and the only defense is to make the bias visible.

## 5. GAAC: Lossless KV Compression for Bandwidth-Efficient LLM Inference on Personal Devices

- **来源**: ArXiv
- **链接**: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38210v1
- **概述**: GAAC提出一种无损KV压缩算法，显著减少LLM推理时的显存占用与带宽需求，提升个人设备上大模型运行的效率。
- **摘要**: Weight-only, lossy quantization substantially reduces the storage and memory requirements for serving large language models (LLMs), but the growing key-value (KV) cache remains a bottleneck for long-context inference on personal devices. We present Group-Adaptive Additive Codec (GAAC), a lossless KV compression and serving system that exploits exponent redundancy to reduce storage and accelerate attention without introducing KV approximation. GAAC encodes BF16 exponents using calibrated per-channel base-plus-offset codes over contiguous ranges. GAAC adapts code widths across small groups while preserving KV values bit-for-bit. A fused attention kernel reconstructs KV tiles on the fly, avoiding full-cache decompression. Block-wise compression and fixed-capacity slots support incremental cache updates and paged serving. Across five tested models, GAAC achieves 1.405-1.407$\times$ lossless BF16 KV compression. We also study compression performance on low-precision tensors FP8 E4M3 caches ( 1.09-1.14$\times$) and E5M2 caches (1.25-1.30$\times$). On NVIDIA DGX Spark, GAAC accelerates decode attention at a 64K-token context by 1.19-1.27$\times$ and end-to-end W4A16 decode steps by 1.04-1.15$\times$. In serving, it reduces resident KV memory by approximately 26% and increases aggregate throughput by 13-29% under comparable KV-memory budgets. On Apple M4, GAAC achieves 1.02-1.25$\times$ attention speedups, demonstrating portability beyond NVIDIA GPUs.
